## 2. Simulated experiments: power, false alarms, peeking

```text
  SIMULATED: control 10 %, test 12 %, 3,835 leads per group, 2,000 experiments each
  situation                                                  significant  should be
  Real effect, planned sample size (= power)                         80%      ~80 %
  No effect at all, A/A test (= false alarm rate)                     5%       ~5 %
  Real effect, only 1/4 of the sample (underpowered)                 30%           
  No effect, but checked 20 times and stopped at first p<0.05         26%       ~5 %

  Lessons:
  - With the planned sample size, a real effect is found about 4 times out of 5.
  - Too small a test misses most real effects. 'Not significant' then means
    'we don't know', not 'there is no effect'.
  - Peeking at the result every few days and stopping when it looks good
    turns a 5 % false alarm rate into about 26%. Decide the sample size first.
```

## 3. How to read one finished test

```text
  SIMULATED example of one finished test (true effect: 10 % -> 12 %)
  A (control): 394 of 3,835 = 10.3%
  B (test):    470 of 3,835 = 12.3%
  Difference B - A: +2.0% points (relative +19%), 95% CI +0.6% to +3.4%, p = 0.0061
  Decision rule (set BEFORE the test): ship B only if the whole interval is above 0 -> ship B
  Before shipping, also check the guardrails: did activation or cancellations get worse
  in group B? A higher win rate with worse sellers is not a success.
```
